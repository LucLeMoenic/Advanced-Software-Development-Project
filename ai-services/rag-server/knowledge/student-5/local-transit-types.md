# Local Transit Types
Last reviewed: 2026-09. Curated demonstration content for the Travel Logistics & Advisory Service: general, non-time-sensitive guidance for Australian passport holders only. It is not legal, migration or medical advice; confirm current rules with official sources before travelling.

Metro and subway systems are usually the fastest way to cross a large city. Most metro networks sell stored-value transit cards or accept contactless bank cards, which avoids buying a paper ticket for every metro trip and often gives a cheaper fare.

Rail and intercity trains connect cities and regions. Some countries sell tourist rail passes that are cheaper when bought before arrival, while others use reserved seats that sell out at peak times, so check whether intercity rail tickets need booking in advance.

Buses reach areas without rail, including suburbs, beaches and rural towns. Bus timetables can be less frequent at night and on weekends, and some bus networks require exact change or a transit card bought before boarding.

Rideshare and taxis: rideshare apps operate in many cities but are restricted or unavailable in some countries. Where rideshare is unavailable, use licensed taxis from official ranks, agree on the meter before departure, and avoid unofficial drivers who approach you in arrival halls.

Ferries link islands, harbours and coastal towns. Ferry services can be cancelled or delayed by rough seas and storms, especially in wet or cyclone season, so leave buffer time before a flight that depends on a ferry connection.

Airport links are dedicated trains, express buses or shuttles between the airport and the city centre. An airport link is usually cheaper and more predictable than a taxi in peak traffic, and many airport links accept the same transit card as the city network.
