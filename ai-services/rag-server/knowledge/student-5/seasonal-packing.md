# Seasonal Weather and Packing
Last reviewed: 2026-09. Curated demonstration content for the Travel Logistics & Advisory Service: general, non-time-sensitive guidance for Australian passport holders only. It is not legal, migration or medical advice; confirm current rules with official sources before travelling.

Opposite seasons: travellers from Australia heading to the northern hemisphere should remember the seasons are reversed. A July trip from Australia to Europe, Japan or North America means summer heat at the destination, while a December trip means winter cold, short days and possible snow.

Packing layers: spring and autumn often bring mild days and cold evenings, so packing layers, such as a light jacket over breathable clothing, handles temperature swings better than one heavy coat. Layers also help with air-conditioned transport and indoor venues.

Wet season and storms: tropical destinations commonly have a wet season with heavy afternoon rain, humidity and occasional cyclones or typhoons. In wet season pack a compact umbrella or rain jacket, quick-dry clothing and insect repellent, and allow buffer days because storms can disrupt flights and ferries.

Hot and cold weather packing: in hot, humid weather pack sun protection, a hat, light breathable clothing and a refillable water bottle. In cold weather pack thermal layers, gloves, a warm hat and waterproof shoes, and check whether heating, snow chains or winter tyres matter for your plans.
