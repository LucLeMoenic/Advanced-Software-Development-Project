# Visa Requirement Categories
Last reviewed: 2026-09. Curated demonstration content for the Travel Logistics & Advisory Service: general, non-time-sensitive guidance for Australian passport holders only. It is not legal, migration or medical advice; confirm current rules with official sources before travelling.

Visa-free entry means an Australian passport holder can enter for a short tourist stay without applying for a visa in advance. Visa-free entry still has conditions: a maximum length of stay, a valid passport, and sometimes proof of onward travel or funds. Visa-free does not mean permission to work or study.

Visa on arrival means the visa is issued at the border, usually at a major airport, after you land. A visa on arrival often requires a fee, a passport with enough validity and blank pages, and an onward or return ticket. Queues for a visa on arrival can be long, so allow extra time after landing.

An eVisa is an electronic visa applied for online before departure. An eVisa approval is linked to your passport number, so the passport you travel on must match the application. Apply for an eVisa well before booking flights, carry a printed copy of the eVisa approval, and check which entry ports the eVisa allows.

An embassy visa must be lodged in advance through an embassy, consulate or visa application centre, sometimes in person with biometrics. An embassy visa usually needs a full itinerary, confirmed accommodation and supporting documents, and processing can take weeks, so an embassy visa should be started first when planning.

Electronic travel authorisations such as the United States ESTA, the New Zealand NZeTA and the United Kingdom ETA are not visas, but they must be approved before you board. A destination can be visa-free and still require an electronic travel authorisation, so treat the authorisation as a mandatory pre-departure step.
