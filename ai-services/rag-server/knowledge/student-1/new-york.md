# New York — Where to Stay
Last reviewed: 2026-09. Curated demonstration content for the Accommodation Recommender: general, non-time-sensitive guidance only. It contains no live prices, availability, events or guarantees; check official sources before travelling.

New York overview: New York is a fast, dense city of five boroughs, with Manhattan at the centre of most visits. It offers museums, theatre, parks and food from every culture, and neighbourhoods change character within a few blocks.

New York who it suits: New York suits couples chasing shows and dining, families who plan around museums and parks, budget travellers willing to stay in outer boroughs, and nightlife fans who enjoy late bars in the East Village, Lower East Side or Brooklyn. The city's rooms are small.

New York safety: New York is generally safe in the main visitor areas, especially busy Manhattan neighbourhoods. Here, stay aware on quiet late-night streets and subway platforms, keep phones secure in crowds, and use licensed taxis or rideshare apps. Safety for tourists in the city depends on normal precautions: stay alert and keep valuables secure to stay safe.

New York travelling with children: New York is fun with kids and children thanks to Central Park, playgrounds and hands-on museums. Families in the city are wise to book rooms with space for everyone, expect many subway stations without lifts, and plan rest breaks because days here are busy.

New York cost and budget: New York is one of the most expensive cities for accommodation. Staying on a budget is easier by staying in Queens, Brooklyn or near a subway line outside Midtown, eating cheap slices and deli meals, and using free parks and ferries.

New York best time to visit: New York is most comfortable in spring and autumn, with mild weather and colourful parks. The city's summer is hot and humid, while winter is cold with occasional snow, festive lights in December and quieter weeks after the holidays.

New York neighbourhoods to stay in: New York first-timers often choose Midtown for sights and theatre access, the Upper West Side for a quiet, family-friendly base near Central Park, Greenwich Village or SoHo for character, and Brooklyn or Long Island City for value. Each neighbourhood suits a different trip, so match the area where you stay to your plans.

New York getting around: New York is best explored on foot and by subway, which runs day and night. The city's Manhattan streets follow a grid that makes walking easy, while taxis in the city are handy with luggage but slow in traffic. A walkable base near public transport makes getting around easier.
