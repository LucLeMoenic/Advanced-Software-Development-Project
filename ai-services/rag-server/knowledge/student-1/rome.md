# Rome — Where to Stay
Last reviewed: 2026-09. Curated demonstration content for the Accommodation Recommender: general, non-time-sensitive guidance only. It contains no live prices, availability, events or guarantees; check official sources before travelling.

Rome overview: Rome is an ancient city where ruins, churches and piazzas sit side by side. Rome historic centre is compact and best enjoyed slowly, and Rome visitors often spend days walking between famous sights.

Rome who it suits: Rome suits couples who love history and long dinners, families interested in ancient sites, budget travellers who stay near Termini, and nightlife fans who enjoy evenings in Trastevere or Monti. Rome is less suited to travellers who dislike walking on cobblestones.

Rome safety: Rome is generally safe for visitors, but pickpockets target crowded buses, the metro and major Rome sights. In Rome, keep bags closed, be wary of distraction scams, and take normal care around Termini station at night. Safety for tourists in Rome depends on normal precautions: stay alert and keep valuables secure to stay safe.

Rome travelling with children: Rome can be great with kids and children who enjoy gladiators, gelato and open piazzas. Families in Rome are wise to plan short sightseeing sessions, pack for cobblestones rather than rely on strollers, and choose rooms with air conditioning for hot Rome afternoons.

Rome cost and budget: Rome accommodation ranges from cheap guesthouses near Termini to expensive boutique hotels in the historic centre. Rome on a budget is easier with pizza by the slice, free churches and piazzas, and a base near a metro or tram line.

Rome best time to visit: Rome is most pleasant in spring and autumn, with warm days and fewer crowds. Rome summer is very hot and busy, and Rome winter is mild and quieter, with shorter queues at famous sights.

Rome neighbourhoods to stay in: Rome first-timers often choose the historic centre near the Pantheon for walkability, Trastevere for atmosphere and nightlife, Monti for a local, central feel, Prati for a quiet base near the Vatican, and Termini for budget and transport links. Each Rome neighbourhood suits a different trip, so match the area where you stay to your plans.

Rome getting around: Rome historic centre is very walkable, and most famous sights sit within a comfortable walk of each other. Rome metro has limited lines, so buses and trams fill the gaps, while taxis in Rome are useful late at night or with luggage. A walkable base near public transport makes getting around easier.
