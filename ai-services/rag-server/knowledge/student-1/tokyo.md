# Tokyo — Where to Stay
Last reviewed: 2026-09. Curated demonstration content for the Accommodation Recommender: general, non-time-sensitive guidance only. It contains no live prices, availability, events or guarantees; check official sources before travelling.

Tokyo overview: Tokyo is a huge, orderly city made of many distinct neighbourhoods rather than one centre. Tokyo rewards travellers who pick a base near a rail line, because most sightseeing days in Tokyo start and end at a station.

Tokyo who it suits: Tokyo suits families who value clean, predictable streets, couples who enjoy food and design, budget travellers willing to stay in compact rooms, and nightlife fans who head to Shinjuku or Shibuya after dark. Tokyo is less suited to travellers who want large rooms.

Tokyo safety: Tokyo is generally considered a very safe city, including late at night and on public transport. Tokyo still has crowded stations where you are wise to watch your bags, and busy nightlife streets where you are wise to decline unsolicited bar invitations. Safety for tourists in Tokyo depends on normal precautions: stay alert and keep valuables secure to stay safe.

Tokyo travelling with children: Tokyo is easy with kids and children because trains are frequent, streets are clean and many stations have lifts. Families in Tokyo are wise to choose rooms that fit everyone, because standard Tokyo rooms are small, and avoid peak rush hours with prams.

Tokyo cost and budget: Tokyo can be done on a budget with business hotels, capsule-style stays and cheap convenience-store or noodle meals. Tokyo becomes expensive in large rooms, central luxury hotels and taxis; a stored-value transit card keeps Tokyo transport costs predictable.

Tokyo best time to visit: Tokyo is most popular in spring for cherry blossom and in autumn for mild, clear weather. Tokyo summer is hot and humid with occasional typhoons, while Tokyo winter is cold, dry and often sunny, with fewer crowds.

Tokyo neighbourhoods to stay in: Tokyo first-timers often stay in Shinjuku for rail links and nightlife, Ginza or Tokyo Station for a central, polished base, Asakusa or Ueno for a quieter, traditional and budget-friendly feel, and Shibuya for shopping and a lively atmosphere. Each Tokyo neighbourhood suits a different trip, so match the area where you stay to your plans.

Tokyo getting around: Tokyo is best explored by train and metro, which are punctual and well signed in English. Tokyo neighbourhoods are walkable once you arrive, but distances between them are long, so choose Tokyo accommodation within a short walk of a station. A walkable base near public transport makes getting around easier.
